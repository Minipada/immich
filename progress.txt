Issue #4: Performance gate: measure model throughput and confirm model choice

Measured whether faster-whisper-small at int8 is fast enough on target
hardware to keep automatic subtitles worth having, and applied the issue's
decision rule. Gate CLOSED: the model selection stands, no escalation.

Benchmark harness (machine-learning/scripts/benchmark_transcription.py):
- Drives the production `WhisperTranscriber` and the server's exact audio
  extraction flags (`-vn -ac 1 -ar 16000 -f wav`, mirroring
  `MediaRepository.extractAudio`), so it measures the path the job queue
  takes rather than a hand-tuned best case.
- Reports throughput as a ratio against real time, loaded-model RSS,
  VAD-detected speech seconds, and a VAD-on/VAD-off comparison. Exits
  non-zero below the 2x threshold so the gate is mechanical.
- Documented under a new "Transcription Throughput" section in
  machine-learning/README.md.

Hardware: Intel i7-8650U (2018 mobile, 4c/8t), 23 GiB RAM, CPU only, at the
server's default 4 threads and queue concurrency 1. Load average 5.9-6.5
during runs, so the numbers are a floor rather than a best case.

Results (production config, VAD on):
- Amateur vlog, 940s, 88.6% speech ......  3.97x real time
- Wedding montage, 689s, 0% speech ...... 127.58x real time
- LibriVox chapter, 553s, 97.9% speech ..   3.51x real time
Three independent repetitions; worst production observation anywhere 2.88x,
still 44% above the 2x threshold.

Model memory footprint: 397 MiB RSS over a ~261 MiB interpreter baseline,
reproducible to 0.1 MiB across three clips and five isolated repetitions.
Peak process RSS during transcription scales with clip length: 940 MiB at
689s, 1458 MiB at 940s.

Effect of VAD: 65.5x faster on the music-only montage (VAD eliminates 100%
of decode work), 1.28x on the talking-heavy vlog, 1.19x on continuous
speech. Two findings worth keeping: with VAD off the montage drops to
1.95x, the only sub-threshold figure measured anywhere, so VAD is
load-bearing rather than an optimisation; and with VAD off the model
hallucinates over non-speech, emitting 24 segments across a clip that
contains none. `vad_filter=True` is a correctness measure as much as a
performance one.

Why the gate is closed on a bound rather than an anecdote: decode cost
tracks detected speech seconds, not media duration. The clips fit
`wall ~ 0.0078 x duration + speech_seconds / 3.5` (the zero-speech montage
isolates the VAD scan cost; the two speech-bearing clips agree to within 3%
on decode rate). Since no video exceeds 100% speech, the floor is ~3.4x, and
every personal video — having less speech to decode — sits above it.

On test material: the issue asks for representative personal video, not a
continuous-speech clip. That proved to be the hard part. Of four archive.org
candidates, one had no audio stream, one had an AAC track carrying no
signal, and the 11.5-minute amateur wedding video — genuine audio, RMS 0.081
— contains zero VAD-detected speech end to end. It is kept in the set
because it is real and representative, not despite it. The continuous-speech
clip is included precisely because it measures the conservative direction.

Recorded in machine-learning/docs/transcription-performance-gate.md, with
raw per-clip measurements in machine-learning/docs/transcription-gate-results/.

Not done: single host, CPU only. A NAS-class Celeron or low-end ARM board
could plausibly fall below 2x and is not covered; anyone targeting such
hardware should re-run the harness. Only English-language material was
measured. No chunking of long audio was added — peak RSS of ~1.5 GiB on a
15-minute video is the strongest argument for the chunking the tracer bullet
deferred, but that is a separate slice.
